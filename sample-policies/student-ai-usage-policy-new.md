# Northbridge College Responsible Student Use of Generative AI Policy

**Version:** 2.0 (New)  
**Effective date:** 1 August 2026  
**Policy owner:** Academic Affairs Division

## 1. Permission model

Generative AI use in a graded assignment is **conditionally allowed** when the published assessment brief labels the task “AI-assisted use permitted” and describes the permitted functions. If the brief is silent, use is not permitted. AI use remains prohibited in closed-book examinations, viva responses, and tasks explicitly labelled “independent work only.”

## 2. Disclosure and evidence

For a permitted task, the student must add an AI-use statement naming the tool, date, purpose, and material influence on the submitted work. Material prompts and outputs must be retained until the appeal period ends and supplied on request. AI output is not an authoritative source; factual claims require independently verifiable sources.

## 3. Data protection

Students must use a college-approved service when personal data, confidential information, unpublished research, or licensed course content could be processed. Sensitive personal data and unpublished examination material must never be entered into a generative AI service. A student must report an accidental disclosure to the service desk within 24 hours.

## 4. Attribution and authorship

The student remains accountable for accuracy, bias, copyright compliance, and the final submission. Fabricated citations, undisclosed generated passages, or use beyond the assessment permission is academic misconduct. A disclosure does not cure a prohibited use.

## 5. Review of suspected misuse

Automated AI-detection output cannot be the sole evidence of misconduct. The course teacher documents textual or process evidence and invites the student to explain their drafts. The Academic Integrity Panel, rather than the individual teacher, decides a contested case.

## 6. Responsibility

The module leader assigns an AI-use label to every assessment before it is released. Academic departments review labels for consistency. The Information Technology and Data Protection Office publishes the approved-service register. Academic Affairs audits a sample of briefs each semester.
